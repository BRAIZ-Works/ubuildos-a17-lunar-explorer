Day 8 of building in public: an engineering project can look finished on a screen long before the real-world evidence exists.

The CAD can be complete.
The drawings can be complete.
The simulation can be complete.
And the rocket can still never have been built or flown.

So this side release is built around one simple question:

**Can the digital engineering be released without pretending physical validation already happened?**

A17 Lunar Explorer is an original lunar-exploration-inspired educational model-rocket project. The admitted digital engineering package includes build documentation, CAD/print files, drawings, BOM, modeled simulation artifacts, graphics, and provenance records.

No fabrication.
No physical validation.
No flight validation.
No guaranteed performance.
No NASA affiliation, endorsement, approval, official status, or official-replica claim.

That isn't the claim.

The larger UBuildOS test is whether a completed engineering subject can move through public release without losing the boundary between what the evidence proves and what still has to happen in the physical world.

**What Day 8 / X01 proves**
- the admitted digital engineering scope reached its prior qualified completion state;
- the public package can expose CAD, documents, BOM, modeled results, and provenance without exposing private controller evidence;
- modeled results stay labeled modeled;
- public claims can stay narrower than the artifact inventory.

**What it does NOT prove**
- that the rocket has been fabricated;
- that it has passed physical or flight validation;
- that modeled performance will occur in the real world;
- that NASA is affiliated with or endorses the project.

The release review reinforced a simple rule: digital completion and physical validation are different lifecycle states. Keep both visible.

Inspect the engineering package. Challenge the evidence boundary.
